The Rout laboratory investigates the structure, assembly, and function of the nuclear pore complex (NPC)—the gateway through which all molecular traffic between the nucleus and cytoplasm must pass. With a mass of approximately 120 MDa and comprising ~34 different protein subunits (nucleoporins) present in multiple copies, the NPC is one of the largest and most elaborate molecular machines in the cell. Using an integrative structural biology approach combining cryo-electron tomography, cross-linking mass spectrometry, proteomics, and computational modeling, the lab has built atomic-resolution models of the entire NPC. Beyond structure, the group also investigates how nucleoporins regulate gene expression and genome organization, and how NPC dysfunction contributes to aging, cancer, and neurodegenerative diseases.
