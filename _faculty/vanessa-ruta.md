The Ruta laboratory seeks to understand how the brain translates sensory information into adaptive behaviors—and how innate behavioral programs are modified by experience. Using Drosophila as a model system, the lab combines electrophysiology, two-photon calcium imaging, optogenetics, and anatomical circuit tracing to map the neural circuits that underlie odor-guided courtship and other innate behaviors. A key research direction investigates how a single fixed neural circuit can produce both stereotyped innate responses and learned modifications of those responses, probing the synaptic and cellular mechanisms by which plasticity is layered onto genetically hard-wired programs. The lab also develops and applies chemical biology tools, including novel molecular sensors and optogenetic actuators, to monitor and perturb neural activity with high spatial and temporal precision in behaving animals.
