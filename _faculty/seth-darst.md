The Darst laboratory (co-PI with Elizabeth Campbell) uses structural biology to elucidate the mechanisms of bacterial transcription and its regulation. Bacterial RNA polymerase (RNAP) is the central enzyme of gene expression, and understanding its intricate molecular choreography—from promoter recognition through elongation and termination—is fundamental to all of microbiology. The lab pioneered the use of electron crystallography and, later, cryo-electron microscopy to determine structures of RNAP and transcription complexes at near-atomic resolution. Key accomplishments include atomic structures of sigma-factor-bound RNAP initiation complexes, RNAP paused during elongation, and RNAP complexed with clinically important antibiotic inhibitors. These structural frameworks guide the rational design of new antibiotics against drug-resistant pathogens.
