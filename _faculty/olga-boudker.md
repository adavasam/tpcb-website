The Boudker laboratory investigates the molecular mechanisms by which membrane transporters move solutes across cellular and intracellular membranes, with a particular focus on excitatory amino acid transporters (EAATs) that remove glutamate from synaptic clefts in the brain. Glutamate clearance is critical for preventing excitotoxicity, and EAAT dysfunction has been linked to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, epilepsy, and other neurological conditions. Using X-ray crystallography and cryo-electron microscopy, the lab has determined structures of archaeal and human glutamate transporters in multiple conformational states, revealing the elevator-like mechanism that alternates solute access between extracellular and intracellular compartments. Current work extends to the pharmacology and regulation of human EAATs, with the goal of identifying allosteric sites that could be targeted by new neuroactive drugs.
