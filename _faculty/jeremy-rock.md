The Rock laboratory studies Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb) biology to identify new antibiotic targets and resistance mechanisms with the goal of shortening tuberculosis treatment. TB remains one of the deadliest infectious diseases globally, and existing drug regimens require months of therapy, fueling non-compliance and resistance. The lab has developed powerful CRISPRi-based genetic tools that enable systematic, quantitative exploration of Mtb gene function—including conditional knockdowns that reveal essential genes and drug-target interactions under physiologically relevant conditions like hypoxia or during infection. Current projects include mapping genetic interaction networks across the Mtb genome, elucidating mechanisms of tolerance to existing antibiotics, and identifying chemical vulnerabilities in Mtb metabolic pathways that could be exploited by next-generation drugs.
