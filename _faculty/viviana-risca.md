The Risca laboratory investigates the three-dimensional organization of chromatin—the complex of DNA and histone proteins—and how nuclear architecture regulates transcriptional programs in development and disease. At the nucleosome level, the lab examines how the positioning and chemical modifications of histones shape chromatin accessibility and gene expression. At the scale of chromosomal domains, the group studies how enhancers and silencers communicate across large genomic distances, and how topological compartments influence the maintenance or aberrant silencing of developmental regulators. The lab develops and applies genomic methods—including MNase-seq, ATAC-seq, and chromosome conformation capture techniques—to map chromatin organization, and combines these with functional genetic perturbations to reveal mechanistic principles of transcriptional repression.
