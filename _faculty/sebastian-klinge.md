The Klinge laboratory focuses on understanding the molecular mechanisms of ribosome biogenesis in eukaryotes—specifically, how the cell assembles the ribosomal subunits that are essential for all protein synthesis. Ribosome assembly is a remarkably complex, quality-controlled process involving more than 200 biogenesis factors that act sequentially to fold ribosomal RNA, incorporate ribosomal proteins, and test functionality before a ribosome is cleared for translation. Using cryo-electron microscopy, the lab captures transient ribosome assembly intermediates at near-atomic resolution, revealing how each successive factor remodels the nascent particle. Defects in ribosome biogenesis underlie a class of human diseases called ribosomopathies, including Diamond-Blackfan anemia, and the lab's structural insights provide a foundation for understanding and targeting these disorders. Dr. Klinge holds the Lenore C. Ford Professorship and directs the Laboratory of Protein and Nucleic Acid Chemistry.
