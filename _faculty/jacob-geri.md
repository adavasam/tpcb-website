The Geri laboratory develops new chemical tools to study and manipulate protein-protein interactions and cellular signaling networks with high spatiotemporal resolution. A key technology pioneered by the group is optically controlled proximity labeling: by engineering light-activated enzymes that biotinylate proteins within nanometer distances of an anchor, the lab can take rapid 'snapshots' of the protein interaction landscape at defined subcellular locations or during specific biological events. This platform enables high-throughput, unbiased discovery of the protein neighborhoods that assemble around receptors, organelles, and signaling hubs in response to stimuli. Complementary research develops new reactive chemistry for covalent probe synthesis and site-selective protein bioconjugation, expanding the chemical toolkit available for proteomics and cell biology.
