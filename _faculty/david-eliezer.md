The Eliezer laboratory investigates the structural properties and biological roles of intrinsically disordered proteins and protein regions, with particular focus on proteins implicated in neurodegenerative diseases. The lab uses solution NMR spectroscopy—a technique uniquely suited to capturing the transient structures and dynamics of disordered polypeptides—to characterize the conformational ensembles of alpha-synuclein (Parkinson's), tau (Alzheimer's and frontotemporal dementia), and related amyloidogenic proteins. A central goal is understanding how these proteins transition from functional monomers to toxic oligomers and amyloid fibrils, and how interactions with lipid membranes, small molecules, and post-translational modifications modulate their aggregation. These mechanistic insights guide the design of inhibitors that stabilize native conformations or disrupt early aggregation intermediates.
