The Campbell laboratory uses structural and biochemical approaches to understand gene expression in bacteria and clinically important bacterial pathogens, including Mycobacterium tuberculosis and coronaviruses. A central focus is the multi-subunit DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RNAP) and the diverse regulatory factors—sigma factors, transcription activators, and inhibitors—that control when and where genes are transcribed. The lab employs cryo-electron microscopy, X-ray crystallography, and biochemical reconstitution to capture RNAP complexes in distinct functional states. These structural insights inform the design of novel antibiotics that target bacterial transcription, an approach with particular urgency given rising antimicrobial resistance.
