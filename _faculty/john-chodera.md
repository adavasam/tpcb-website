The Chodera laboratory develops and applies computation and high-throughput experiments to build quantitative, multiscale models of how small molecules interact with biomolecular targets and alter cellular pathways. The group pioneers open-source tools for alchemical free-energy calculations—methods that predict binding affinities from physics-based models with high accuracy—and GPU-accelerated molecular dynamics simulations. These computational approaches are coupled with automated biophysical experiments (including rapid mixing and single-molecule methods) to characterize drug-target interactions and resistance mutations. The Chodera lab is a core member of the Open Force Field Initiative, the Folding@home Consortium, and the AI-driven ASAP antiviral discovery platform, exemplifying a commitment to open, reproducible computational drug discovery.
