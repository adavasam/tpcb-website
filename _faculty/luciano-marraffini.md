The Marraffini laboratory studies the biology of CRISPR-Cas systems in bacteria, with a particular focus on understanding how these immune systems defend against bacteriophage infection and horizontal gene transfer. Professor Marraffini's group made foundational contributions to establishing that CRISPR-Cas systems can target DNA—a discovery critical to the development of CRISPR-based genome editing technologies. Current research investigates the molecular mechanisms by which diverse CRISPR-Cas systems acquire new spacers from invading nucleic acids (adaptation), recognize and destroy foreign DNA or RNA (interference), and are regulated to avoid autoimmunity. The lab also explores the evolutionary arms race between bacteria and phage, including phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, to understand how immunity evolves in natural microbial communities.
