The Alushin lab studies how cells sense and respond to mechanical forces through changes in cytoskeletal architecture. Cells are constantly subjected to tension and compression from their environment and from their own internal machinery; the lab investigates how these forces cause actin filaments to change conformation, and how specialized actin-binding proteins 'read' those structural changes to transduce mechanical cues into biochemical signaling pathways. To visualize these events at atomic resolution, the group develops cryo-electron microscopy methods capable of capturing macromolecules under mechanical load, and combines these structural approaches with fluorescence imaging in cells. Long-term goals include understanding how mechanosensory defects contribute to cancer, fibrosis, and developmental disease.
