The Scheuring laboratory uses high-speed atomic force microscopy (HS-AFM) to visualize membrane proteins and their dynamics in near-physiological conditions at nanometer spatial and millisecond temporal resolution. Unlike cryo-EM or X-ray crystallography, which capture static snapshots, HS-AFM enables direct observation of protein motions—including conformational changes in ion channels, the assembly and disassembly of membrane protein arrays, and lipid bilayer remodeling—in real time. The lab has pioneered HS-AFM imaging of intact membrane patches containing transporters, channels, and receptors in native-like lipid environments. Current research targets mechanosensitive ion channels, SARS-CoV-2 spike protein dynamics on viral membranes, and the relationship between protein oligomeric state and function.
