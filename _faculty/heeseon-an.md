The An laboratory uses chemical biology and proteomics to study protein homeostasis—the cellular mechanisms that ensure proteins are properly folded, assembled, and degraded. A particular focus is the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) and selective autophagy pathways that degrade misfolded or excess proteins, and how these systems are rewired in cancer cells to support proliferation and stress resistance. The lab develops chemical probes and activity-based proteomic approaches to map the activity landscape of E3 ubiquitin ligases and deubiquitinases across the proteome, enabling discovery of new regulatory mechanisms and potential drug targets. Additional research explores how the molecular chaperone network coordinates with degradation pathways to maintain proteostasis and how disruption of these processes drives cancer and neurodegenerative disease.
