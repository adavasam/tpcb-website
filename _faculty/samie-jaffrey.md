The Jaffrey laboratory works at the interface of chemical biology and RNA biology, developing new tools to visualize and manipulate RNA in living cells. The group invented the Spinach and Broccoli families of RNA aptamers that bind and activate small-molecule fluorophores, creating 'RNA-fluorophore' complexes analogous to fluorescent proteins but for RNA imaging. These genetically encodable fluorescent RNA tags enable real-time tracking of specific RNA molecules in cells without the need for antibodies or hybridization probes. Beyond imaging, the lab applies aptamer technology to develop riboswitch-based gene regulation systems and to study the role of RNA modifications (the 'epitranscriptome'), especially m6A methylation, in controlling mRNA stability, translation, and neuronal function. Current work also investigates how altered RNA regulation contributes to neurodevelopmental disorders, autism, and cancer, combining molecular biology, chemical biology, imaging, and bioinformatics approaches.
