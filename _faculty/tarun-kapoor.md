The Kapoor laboratory sits at the interface of chemistry and cell biology, developing and applying small-molecule probes to dissect the mechanisms of the mitotic spindle and other essential cellular machines. A hallmark of the group's work is the use of chemical genetics—designing and deploying cell-permeable inhibitors with exquisite selectivity—to perturb specific cytoskeletal motors, kinases, and ATPases at defined moments in the cell cycle. Recent research has focused on the molecular mechanisms of VCP/p97, an AAA+ ATPase critical for protein homeostasis and a promising cancer drug target, including allosteric small-molecule activation and conformational control. The lab also develops approaches to identify physiologically relevant drug targets by mapping the cellular consequences of probe action at the systems level, and directs the Pels Family Center for Biochemistry and Structural Biology at Rockefeller.
