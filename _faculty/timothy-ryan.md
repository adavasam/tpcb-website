The Ryan laboratory investigates the physical chemistry and bioenergetics of the synapse, focusing on how presynaptic nerve terminals maintain their capacity for rapid, repeated neurotransmitter release. Synaptic transmission is energetically demanding; each action potential triggers a burst of exocytosis and subsequent membrane retrieval that must be powered by mitochondrial ATP and local calcium buffering. The lab uses genetically encoded fluorescent sensors, super-resolution microscopy, and electrophysiology to measure ATP, calcium, pH, and vesicle cycling dynamics in individual synaptic boutons with millisecond time resolution. Current research addresses how mitochondrial positioning, cristae remodeling, and lipid metabolism are regulated to match energy supply with the variable demands of synaptic activity—a balance that fails in neurodegeneration and metabolic disease.
