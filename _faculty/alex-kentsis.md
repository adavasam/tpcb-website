The Kentsis laboratory applies functional proteomics, chemical biology, and developmental cancer biology to understand and treat cancers that affect children and young adults, including embryonal tumors, leukemias, brain tumors, and sarcomas. A key research direction is defining proteome vulnerabilities of cancer cells—the specific proteins and complexes that cancer cells depend on for survival—using quantitative mass spectrometry, CRISPR genetic screens, and drug sensitivity profiling. The lab also investigates how oncoproteins formed by chromosomal translocations (such as NUP98 fusions) rewire transcription and protein interactions to drive transformation. Computational biology and biophysical approaches complement experimental work to identify new druggable targets. Dr. Kentsis directs the Tow Center for Developmental Oncology and practices as a pediatric hematologist-oncologist at MSK Kids.
