The Andersen laboratory investigates the molecular mechanisms by which small molecules, ions, and lipids influence the function of ion channels and other membrane proteins. A central research theme is how the physical properties of the lipid bilayer—its thickness, curvature stress, and material properties—couple mechanically to the conformational changes of embedded proteins, thereby modulating their gating behavior. The lab uses electro-physiological recording (primarily bilayer reconstitution and patch-clamp) combined with chemical modification and spectroscopic approaches to resolve the contributions of lipid-protein interactions to channel function. These studies have broad implications for understanding anesthetic action, drug effects on excitable tissues, and the biophysical basis of membrane protein pharmacology.
