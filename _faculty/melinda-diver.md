The Diver laboratory investigates the structure and molecular mechanism of somatosensory ion channels—the proteins that transduce painful, thermal, and mechanical stimuli in nociceptive neurons. These channels, including members of the transient receptor potential (TRP) family, are important drug targets for chronic pain, itch, and inflammatory conditions. The lab uses cryo-electron microscopy to determine high-resolution structures of TRP channels in multiple functional states and bound to endogenous ligands, lipid modulators, and clinical drug candidates. By capturing channels in open, closed, inactivated, and drug-bound conformations, the group aims to understand how diverse chemical and physical stimuli converge on a common gate, and how this knowledge can be used to design selective modulators for pain relief.
