The Liu laboratory probes the dynamic interplay between gene expression machineries using single-molecule and nanoscale biophysical approaches. A major focus is understanding how transcription, chromatin remodeling, and replication are coordinated—and how conflicts between these processes are resolved—at the level of individual molecules in real time. The group develops novel optical tweezers and single-molecule fluorescence methods to directly observe the movements of RNA polymerases, helicases, and chromatin factors along their DNA or RNA templates. These experiments reveal fundamental mechanisms underlying gene regulation, genome maintenance, and the pathological dysregulation seen in cancer and other diseases.
