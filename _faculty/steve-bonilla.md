The Bonilla lab investigates the three-dimensional structure and function of RNA molecules that play essential roles in cellular and viral biology. RNAs are not passive carriers of genetic information; they fold into sophisticated architectures that drive catalysis, gene regulation, and viral replication. Using cryo-electron microscopy combined with biochemical and biophysical approaches, the lab determines high-resolution structures of RNA and RNA-protein complexes, with a particular focus on how conformational dynamics regulate function. A major research direction targets the structured RNA elements within RNA virus genomes that coordinate genome packaging, replication, and immune evasion, with the goal of revealing new targets for antiviral therapeutics.
