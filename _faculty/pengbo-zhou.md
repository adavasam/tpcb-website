The Zhou laboratory investigates the physiological and pathological roles of ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation in cancer and development. The ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) is the cell's primary route for regulated protein destruction, and aberrant UPS activity underlies a wide range of malignancies. A central focus of the lab is the CRL2/VHL E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, which targets the hypoxia-inducible factor HIF-1alpha for degradation under normoxic conditions; loss of VHL function causes clear-cell renal cell carcinoma and drives angiogenesis in solid tumors. The lab uses biochemical reconstitution, structural biology, mouse genetics, and chemical probe strategies to dissect substrate recognition by the VHL complex and to identify new protein substrates and E3 ligase complexes that could be targeted for cancer therapy.
