The Tan laboratory applies total synthesis, diversity-oriented synthesis, and chemical genomics to discover new antibiotics and anticancer agents. A central research philosophy is the idea that natural product-inspired scaffold diversity provides access to unexplored biological space: by synthesizing libraries of complex natural product-like compounds, the lab identifies chemical matter capable of modulating challenging biological targets. In the antibiotic area, the lab uses phenotypic screening in Mycobacterium tuberculosis and has identified novel inhibitors of lipid metabolism enzymes. For cancer, the group has developed covalent inhibitors of oncogenic transcription factors and drug resistance-associated transferases that would be inaccessible with conventional drug-like compound libraries.
