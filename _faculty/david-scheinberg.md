The Scheinberg laboratory combines molecular pharmacology, chemical biology, and immunology to develop targeted therapies for cancer. A signature research direction is actinium-225 and bismuth-213-based targeted alpha therapy: by conjugating radioactive isotopes to antibodies that home to cancer-specific antigens, the lab creates radioimmunoconjugates that deliver highly lethal, short-range alpha particle radiation selectively to tumor cells while sparing surrounding tissue. The lab has pioneered translational development of alpha-emitter immunoconjugates from discovery through clinical trials in leukemia and other cancers. Additional research investigates the chemical synthesis and pharmacology of novel antibodies, bispecific antibodies, and peptide-drug conjugates targeting leukemia, lymphoma, and glioblastoma, with a long-term mission of developing cures for liquid tumors.
