The Walz laboratory employs cryo-electron microscopy and cryo-electron tomography to determine the structures of membrane proteins and macromolecular complexes with the goal of understanding the molecular mechanisms of membrane-associated biological processes. A long-standing research focus is the aquaporin family of water channels, including structural studies that revealed how these channels achieve exquisite selectivity for water while excluding ions and other solutes. The lab also investigates lipid-protein interactions, membrane protein dynamics, and the structural basis of membrane fusion and fission. More recently, the group has developed methods for cryo-electron tomography of intact organelles, enabling visualization of macromolecular machines in their native cellular context at near-atomic resolution.
