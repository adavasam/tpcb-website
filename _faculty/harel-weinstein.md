The Weinstein laboratory applies computational biophysics and theoretical approaches to understand the structure-function relationships of membrane proteins, particularly neurotransmitter transporters and G protein-coupled receptors. The lab uses molecular dynamics simulations, free-energy calculations, and structural bioinformatics to model how these proteins change conformation during function, how they interact with lipid bilayers, and how drugs, ions, and modulatory proteins alter their behavior. A long-standing interest is the molecular pharmacology of serotonin and monoamine transporters—targets of antidepressants and drugs of abuse—with the goal of understanding selectivity determinants and designing improved therapeutics. The lab also develops computational frameworks for analyzing high-dimensional simulation data and for integrating simulations with experimental structural and functional constraints.
