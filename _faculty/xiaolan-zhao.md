The Zhao laboratory investigates how the small ubiquitin-like modifier SUMO and associated protein modification pathways regulate genome organization, replication, repair, and cellular stress responses. The lab applies biochemical, genetic, and cell biology approaches—primarily in budding yeast but with extension to mammalian cells—to characterize the substrates and mechanisms of SUMO E3 ligases, as well as the SUMO-targeted ubiquitin ligases (STUbLs) that link sumoylation to proteasomal degradation. A major research direction is how sumoylation regulates the spatial organization of the genome in the nucleus, including the clustering of repetitive DNA elements and the formation of biomolecular condensates at sites of DNA damage. These insights are relevant to understanding how cancer cells survive genotoxic stress.
