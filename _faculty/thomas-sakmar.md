The Sakmar laboratory investigates the molecular mechanisms by which G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) sense signals and relay them across cellular membranes. GPCRs represent the largest family of druggable receptors and are targeted by approximately one-third of all approved pharmaceuticals. The lab uses chemical biology tools—including the incorporation of unnatural amino acids, fluorescence spectroscopy, and NMR—to probe the conformational dynamics of GPCRs in the context of the lipid bilayer as they engage ligands, couple to G proteins, and are regulated by arrestins. A particular focus is the visual receptor rhodopsin and related olfactory and chemokine receptors. The group also applies these mechanistic insights to develop novel biosensors and to understand the biophysical basis of GPCR pharmacology, including functional selectivity ('biased agonism') that may enable safer drugs with fewer side effects.
