The Maxfield laboratory uses biophysical chemistry and live-cell fluorescence imaging to study membrane lipid traffic and the regulation of intracellular cholesterol homeostasis. The endocytic pathway delivers LDL-derived cholesterol from lysosomes to the endoplasmic reticulum and plasma membrane, and the lab has been instrumental in characterizing the sorting events, lipid transfer proteins, and membrane dynamics that govern this process. Disruption of cholesterol trafficking underlies Niemann-Pick type C disease and contributes to atherosclerosis and neurodegeneration. The group employs quantitative fluorescence microscopy, fluorescent cholesterol analogs, and genetic tools to map the spatiotemporal regulation of lipid movement through endosomal compartments, providing mechanistic insight into lysosomal storage disorders.
