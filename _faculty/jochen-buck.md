The Buck laboratory focuses on the biology of soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC), a bicarbonate- and calcium-sensing enzyme that generates the second messenger cAMP in intracellular microdomains distinct from those regulated by G protein-coupled receptor-activated transmembrane adenylyl cyclases. The lab discovered sAC as a sensor of metabolic state and has demonstrated its roles in sperm motility and capacitation, kidney acidification, mitochondrial function, immune cell regulation, and cancer cell survival. Using pharmacological tools, mouse genetics, and cell biology approaches, the group dissects how compartmentalized cAMP signaling coordinates physiological responses ranging from fertilization to carbon dioxide sensing. A key translational goal is developing selective sAC inhibitors as potential contraceptives and modulators of other sAC-dependent pathophysiology.
