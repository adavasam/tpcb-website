The David laboratory uses chemical biology tools to dissect the mechanisms by which histone modifications and chromatin state regulate gene expression in cancer and during aging. A distinctive approach is the use of synthetic biology—including native chemical ligation and expressed protein ligation—to build histone proteins bearing defined post-translational modifications at any combination of sites, enabling precise interrogation of modification cross-talk. These designer histones are used to reconstitute chromatin substrates in vitro and to identify the 'reader' proteins that recognize specific modification combinations. The lab also develops chemical probes that target chromatin regulatory complexes, with a focus on the polycomb repressive complex and other silencing machinery that is frequently dysregulated in cancer.
