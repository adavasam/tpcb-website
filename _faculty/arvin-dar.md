The Dar laboratory investigates the chemical biology and structural basis of kinase regulation in cancer, focusing on mechanisms that can be exploited to overcome drug resistance. A major research direction explores molecular glues and heterobifunctional degraders (PROTACs) that redirect E3 ligases to degrade oncogenic kinases, including RAF and CDK, which are difficult to inhibit conventionally. The lab also studies allosteric mechanisms in kinases—how regulatory inputs at surfaces distant from the active site control catalytic activity and subcellular localization—and develops switchable small-molecule tools to probe these mechanisms in living cells. Structural studies (cryo-EM and X-ray crystallography) combined with chemical proteomics provide a mechanistic framework for understanding targeted protein degradation and designing next-generation cancer therapies.
