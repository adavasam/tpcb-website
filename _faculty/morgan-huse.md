The Huse laboratory investigates the chemical biology of lymphocyte signaling and cytotoxicity, combining imaging technology with synthetic chemistry and materials science to understand how immune cells communicate. The lab uses advanced microscopy (TIRF, lattice light-sheet), optogenetics, and biophysical tools to visualize signaling dynamics at the immunological synapse—the specialized interface formed between a lymphocyte and its target. Key areas of investigation include how diacylglycerol, calcium, and mechanical forces orchestrate T cell receptor signaling, how cytotoxic granules are targeted and released, and how plasma membrane biomechanics of immune cells influence cytolysis and phagocytosis. A particular emphasis is the mechanoregulation of cytotoxic T lymphocyte and NK cell killing, with direct implications for cancer immunotherapy.
