The Shuman laboratory investigates the enzymology and chemical biology of RNA metabolism, with particular focus on RNA capping, ligation, and nucleic acid repair. The lab discovered and characterized the RNA triphosphatase, guanylyltransferase, and methyltransferase enzymes that collaboratively install the m7G cap—a critical co-transcriptional modification that protects mRNAs from exonuclease degradation and promotes their translation. The cap synthesis enzymes from fungi and protozoa are structurally distinct from their mammalian counterparts, making them attractive targets for antifungal and antiparasitic drug development. Parallel work focuses on RNA and DNA ligases in bacteria, yeasts, and DNA repair contexts, illuminating mechanistic principles of nucleic acid end-joining that are exploited by pathogens and are essential for DNA damage responses.
