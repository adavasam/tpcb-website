The MacKinnon laboratory investigates the structure, function, and pharmacology of ion channels and their associated regulatory proteins. Ion channels are pore-forming membrane proteins that control the flow of ions across membranes, making them essential for nerve impulse propagation, muscle contraction, hormone secretion, and countless other physiological processes. Professor MacKinnon was awarded the 2003 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for determining the first crystal structure of a potassium channel, revealing the molecular basis of ion selectivity and permeation. Current research continues to explore the structural basis of ion channel gating—how channels open, close, and are inactivated in response to voltage, ligands, or mechanical stimuli—and how lipids, toxins, and small molecules modulate channel function. These insights have broad implications for understanding and treating epilepsy, cardiac arrhythmias, pain, and neurodegeneration.
