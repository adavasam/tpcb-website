The Pavletich laboratory investigates the structural basis of cell cycle control and its dysregulation in cancer, with a particular emphasis on the ubiquitin-proteasome system that destroys cell cycle regulators at precise times and on the tumor suppressors that prevent inappropriate cell proliferation. The lab has determined landmark crystal structures of the retinoblastoma tumor suppressor protein (Rb) bound to E2F transcription factor domains, cyclin-CDK complexes, and key components of the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C)—the E3 ubiquitin ligase that orchestrates cell cycle exit. These structures have illuminated how CDK phosphorylation relieves Rb-mediated transcriptional repression, and how the APC/C achieves exquisite substrate specificity and temporal control. The lab uses X-ray crystallography and cryo-EM as primary tools.
