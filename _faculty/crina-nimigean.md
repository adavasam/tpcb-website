The Nimigean laboratory studies the mechanisms by which ion channels open and close in response to membrane voltage, ligands, and mechanical stimuli, with a particular focus on potassium channels and cyclic nucleotide-gated channels. Ion channel gating is a fundamental biophysical process underlying nerve impulse propagation, cardiac rhythm, and signal transduction. The lab uses a combination of high-resolution structural methods (X-ray crystallography and cryo-EM), single-channel electrophysiology, and fluorescence spectroscopy to capture channels in distinct functional states and map the conformational pathways that couple sensor domains to pore opening. A long-standing interest is how cyclic nucleotides bind allosterically and propagate conformational changes over long distances within these tetrameric channels, with implications for understanding heart disease, epilepsy, and sensory physiology.
