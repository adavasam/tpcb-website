The Brady lab mines the vast genetic diversity of environmental microbiomes to discover and characterize new genetically encoded small molecules. The majority of Earth's microbiome cannot be cultured in the laboratory, making its extraordinary chemical diversity largely inaccessible by traditional natural products approaches. The lab addresses this challenge through metagenomic methods—extracting and expressing biosynthetic gene clusters directly from soil and human gut DNA without culturing the source organisms. This approach has yielded numerous novel antibiotics, antifungals, and other bioactive compounds. The group also applies synthetic biology to refactor and optimize these pathways, and uses chemical and structural tools to understand how the encoded enzymes build complex molecular scaffolds with high precision.
