The Levitz laboratory investigates the molecular and circuit-level mechanisms underlying neurotransmitter receptor function, with particular emphasis on metabotropic glutamate receptors (mGluRs) and their roles in synaptic plasticity and neurological disease. The lab has pioneered the use of optogenetic pharmacology—attaching photoswitchable ligands to receptors to control their activation with light—enabling precise spatiotemporal manipulation of receptor activity in intact neural circuits. These tools are combined with two-photon imaging, electrophysiology, and structural approaches to reveal how mGluR conformational dynamics encode synaptic state, how receptor dimerization regulates signaling, and how mGluRs can be selectively targeted to modulate specific behaviors relevant to schizophrenia, anxiety, and addiction.
