The Chiosis laboratory is a pioneer in the chemical biology of heat shock proteins (HSPs), particularly HSP90, as scaffolding chaperones that support oncogenic protein complexes. In cancer, HSP90 forms aberrant assemblies called 'epichaperomes'—large, stable complexes of chaperones and co-chaperones that remodel the proteome to enable tumor cell survival under stress. The lab designs and synthesizes purine scaffold-based inhibitors that selectively bind the cancer-specific epichaperome HSP90, and has developed imaging agents that allow non-invasive detection of epichaperome-positive tumors by PET imaging. These tools are being translated into clinical candidates for cancer treatment and have also revealed epichaperome assembly as a driver of neurodegenerative disease, expanding their potential therapeutic applications.
