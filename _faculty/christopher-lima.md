The Lima laboratory investigates the structural basis of ubiquitin-like modifier (SUMO) conjugation and mRNA metabolism, two fundamental processes that are frequently dysregulated in cancer. A major research direction is the molecular mechanism of the SUMO conjugation pathway (sumoylation): the lab has determined landmark crystal structures of the E1, E2, and E3 enzymes that catalyze SUMO transfer to substrates, revealing how specificity is achieved at each step. Sumoylation regulates hundreds of nuclear proteins involved in transcription, DNA repair, and genome organization. Parallel work focuses on the RNA exosome—a multi-subunit 3'-to-5' exoribonuclease that degrades aberrant and regulatory RNAs—including structural characterization of the exosome in complex with its adaptors and substrates.
