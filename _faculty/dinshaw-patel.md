The Patel laboratory is internationally recognized for structural and biophysical studies of nucleic acid structures, chemical modifications, and their interactions with proteins. The lab uses X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, and cryo-electron microscopy to determine atomic structures of DNA and RNA in biologically important conformations—including G-quadruplexes, i-motifs, DNA mismatches, and unusual helical structures—as well as complexes of nucleic acids with small molecules and proteins relevant to transcription, epigenetics, and DNA repair. A notable research direction is the structural characterization of CRISPR-Cas effector complexes, providing atomic details of target recognition and cleavage that inform applications in genome editing and anti-phage defense.
