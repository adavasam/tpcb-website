The Meyer laboratory investigates how cells make decisions about growth, migration, and differentiation in response to environmental signals, with a focus on the spatiotemporal dynamics of phosphoinositide and calcium signaling. The lab has made foundational contributions to understanding PI3K signaling: how phosphatidylinositol-3,4,5-trisphosphate (PIP3) gradients guide cell polarization and directed migration, and how feedback loops within the PI3K-PTEN network generate bistable, all-or-none cell fate decisions. The group uses live-cell FRET biosensors, optogenetic tools, and microfluidics to monitor and perturb signaling dynamics in individual cells with high temporal resolution. Current projects integrate single-cell transcriptomics with live-cell imaging to understand how signaling and gene expression programs are coordinated during development.
