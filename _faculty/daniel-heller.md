The Heller laboratory develops nanotechnologies for cancer diagnosis and treatment, focusing on three core areas: drug delivery technologies that target therapies to disease sites, advanced diagnostics for early-stage cancer detection, and nanosensors to accelerate discovery of new therapies. A central technology platform uses single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs), which emit near-infrared fluorescence that can penetrate deeply into living tissue, as molecular sensors for disease biomarkers. By functionalizing SWCNT surfaces with specific recognition moieties, the lab creates sensors that detect nucleic acids, proteins, and metabolites in biological fluids and inside living cells, enabling real-time monitoring of disease states. Parallel research develops nanoparticle platforms for targeted drug delivery to tumors, including lipid nanoparticles and polymeric nanosystems that can encapsulate and release chemotherapy or nucleic acid therapeutics with spatial and temporal control.
