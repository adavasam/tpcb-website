## A wide range of chemical biology

TPCB students pursue research with the program's faculty across all three campuses, in a wide range of areas of chemical biology. Multidisciplinary research centers foster collaborations between labs, which is what lets students take on problems beyond the scope of any one conventional discipline. An array of state-of-the-art core facilities supplies the specialized equipment and expertise that thesis projects depend on, and students take part in several seminar series in chemical biology and related areas where leading scientists from around the world present their work.

Research in the program is organized around five major research topics and seven therapeutic areas. To see which labs work where, use the [faculty directory]({{ '/faculty/' | relative_url }}) — every faculty member is tagged both by the approaches their lab uses and by the biology it studies, and each profile lists that lab's publications with TPCB students.

## Five research topics

### Bioactive small molecules

Small molecules are powerful chemical tools for studying cellular processes and for pursuing new therapeutic targets. Research here spans the traditional disciplines of organic synthesis, medicinal chemistry, genetics, biochemistry, and analytical chemistry, applied both to fundamental science and to translational work in drug discovery. Student projects take up chemical methodology, total synthesis, rational drug design, library synthesis, natural product biosynthesis, high-throughput screening assays, and chemical probe development.

### Macromolecular structure and function

Detailed mechanistic study of proteins, nucleic acids, and cellular structures. Biochemistry and biophysics merge here to explain the roles these molecules play in cellular processes, and students address those questions with enzymology, X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, cryo-electron microscopy, and single-molecule methods.

### Chemical cell biology

Molecular tools applied to key questions in cellular function, yielding both a fundamental understanding of those processes and insight into modulating them for therapeutic purposes. The work asks students to integrate pharmacology, cell biology, and analytical chemistry; specific projects focus on chemical genetics, cellular imaging, proteomics, metabolomics, and activity-based protein profiling.

### Biotechnology

A burgeoning area, and the route to new biologics, diagnostics, and drug delivery systems for clinical use. Students draw on organic chemistry, materials science, biochemistry, cell biology, and immunology to create proteins, antibodies, nanotubes, and nanoparticles with novel biological properties. Program interests here include protein engineering, nanotechnology, and radiochemistry.

### Computational chemistry

Quantitative, predictive modeling brought to bear on current challenges in drug discovery. Physical chemistry, biochemistry, organic chemistry, applied mathematics, statistics, and computer science and engineering are blended to analyze small molecules, macromolecules, and the interactions between them. Student projects involve topics such as cheminformatics, docking, and protein modeling.

## Seven therapeutic areas

Translational research is a major focus of the program, and gives students the chance to contribute directly both to the understanding of human disease and to the development of novel therapeutics. Students and faculty with a wide range of scientific interests and backgrounds combine their efforts across seven major therapeutic areas:

- Cancer
- Cardiovascular disease
- Diabetes and other metabolic disorders
- Immunology, including antibody and vaccine therapies
- Microbiology and antibiotics
- Neuroscience and neurodegenerative diseases
- Virology

## Collaboration across labs

Multidisciplinary collaborative research is growing quickly in importance, and it asks scientists to be expert in their own discipline while remaining fluent enough to work with colleagues in others. TPCB students are well positioned for that, during their thesis research and after it. A number of students have been jointly mentored by two TPCB faculty members with complementary research programs.

## Mentorship

The program places a strong emphasis on mentorship. All TPCB faculty are required to complete institutional training in mentorship, and to continue that training every year. The three institutions also sponsor a variety of mentor training seminars and workshops throughout the year.
