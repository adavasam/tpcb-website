## Research at the interface of chemistry and biology

TPCB training is built around laboratory research at the forefront of the field. Students begin with three laboratory rotations during their first year, which give them exposure to a range of research topics, techniques, and philosophies before they commit to a thesis laboratory. Rigorous coursework runs alongside the rotations to ensure that every student has the fundamental knowledge base their research will demand, drawn from classes across the Tri-Institutional campuses and at neighboring institutions in New York City.

Students select a thesis laboratory by the end of the first year and begin thesis research. By the end of the second year they must design, write, and defend a thesis research proposal before a committee of TPCB faculty — called the *Admission to Candidacy Exam* at Weill Cornell and Sloan Kettering, and the *Thesis Research Proposal* at Rockefeller. Once that oral examination is passed, students devote their full time to thesis research.

The program sets no arbitrary graduation deadline. Most students complete their studies in approximately five and a half years, in keeping with the national average. Students receive their PhD from the institution where they carry out their thesis research, with the exception of Sloan Kettering students, who receive their degrees from Weill Cornell.

## Before classes begin

Incoming students arrive on campus on **July 1** and formally enroll at Weill Cornell. An August start date is available only to students with extended academic commitments.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| May 1 | Deadline for incoming international students to submit completed I-20 visa applications to TPCB. Kickoff meetings with First-Year Advisors begin, covering scientific interests and potential rotations. |
| June 20 | Deadline for receipt of the final official undergraduate transcript, extended for students whose institutions graduate later. |
| July 1 | Students arrive in New York and enroll, with a welcome lunch and orientation with the TPCB directors and staff. |
| early July | Deadline to enroll in the Columbia School of Professional Studies, for students planning to take classes at Columbia. |
| 3rd week of July | Deadline to arrange Laboratory Rotation #1 directly with a TPCB faculty member, in consultation with the First-Year Advisor. |
| 4th week of July | Deadline to establish an Individual Development Plan (IDP) and meet with the First-Year Advisor to refine it. |
| beginning of August | Laboratory Rotation #1 begins. |
| late August | Graduate Student Orientation Week, hosted by Weill Cornell. |
| late Aug / early Sep | Classes begin on all campuses. |

The whole of July is given over to a dedicated curriculum: orientation meetings with institutional staff, *Meet the Faculty* seminars, seminar course workshops and a first-year journal club with the TPCB directors, rotation lab overview presentations, peer mentoring workshops run by current students, fellowships workshops, and First-Year Advisor meetings to plan rotations, coursework, and the IDP.

## Laboratory rotations

Students must rotate in **at least three different labs, on at least two of the three campuses**. Dates can be adjusted in consultation with the First-Year Advisor and the faculty mentors involved.

| Rotation | When | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation #1 | August – October | 3 months |
| Rotation #2 | November – mid-February | 3.5 months |
| Rotation #3 | mid-February – May | 3.5 months |

Any lab in the [faculty directory]({{ '/faculty/' | relative_url }}) is a possible rotation host.

## Coursework

Students complete **four lecture courses**, normally in years 1 and 2. Two are required of everyone; the third is chosen from a core list and the fourth is an elective.

### Required

1. **TPCB Principles of Chemical Biology** — Rockefeller, Fall
2. **TPCB Chemistry in Biology & Medicine** — Weill Cornell, Spring

### Core course (select one)

- Biochemistry Core — Weill Cornell, Fall
- Biophysical Principles of Molecular & Cellular Systems — Weill Cornell, Fall
- Pharmacology I: Chemical Biology — Weill Cornell, Fall
- Statistical Thermodynamics — Columbia G4230, Fall
- Synthetic Methods in Organic Chemistry I — Columbia G4148, Fall

### Elective (select one, or a second course from the core list)

- Advanced Organic Chemistry — Columbia G4147, Fall
- Biochemical and Biophysical Methods — Rockefeller, Fall, intermittent
- Cancer Pharmacology — Weill Cornell, Spring
- Cell Biology — Rockefeller, Spring, even years
- Cellular and Organismal Metabolism — Rockefeller, Winter
- Cryoelectron Microscopy of Macromolecular Assemblies — NYSBC/NYU 4408, Spring
- Introduction to Biostatistics — Weill Cornell, Spring
- Drug Development: From Molecule to Prescription — Weill Cornell, Spring
- NMR Spectroscopy of Macromolecules — NYSBC/Columbia G6270, Spring, even years

Other electives may be selected with prior approval from the TPCB Program Director.

## Seminars and research meetings

Interactive research seminars run throughout the program, and much of this calendar is organized by the students themselves.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Years 1 & 2 | TPCB Chemical Biology Seminar Course — a seminar journal club, Fall and Spring |
| Years 1 & 5 | Tri-Institutional Responsible Conduct of Research Course — Fall |
| All years | TPCB Student Research in Progress Seminar Series — approximately biweekly |
| All years | [Tri-Institutional Chemical Biology Symposium]({{ '/symposium/' | relative_url }}) — annual |
| All years | TPCB Open House Poster Session — annual |
| All years | TPCB Student Career Development Retreat — annual, off campus |

Each year the second-year class selects, invites, and hosts a speaker in the flagship Sanders Tri-Institutional Chemical Biology Seminar Series. The Student Career Development Retreat combines research talks with career development activities and is as much a social occasion as a scientific one.

## Thesis research and milestones

Students must pass an initial PhD candidacy exam and a final PhD thesis defense.

| Milestone | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Selection of thesis laboratory | June 1, end of Year 1 |
| PhD candidacy exam | June 30, end of Year 2 |
| Thesis Committee meetings | at least annually in Years 3 and 4 |
| Thesis Committee meetings | at least every 6 months from Year 5 through completion |

**External fellowship application.** Every student submits at least one external fellowship application during their training, generally built on the successful thesis proposal. See [external fellowships]({{ '/external-fellowships/' | relative_url }}) for the awards TPCB students most often pursue.

**Thesis defense.** A written thesis, a public oral presentation, and a private defense before the Thesis Committee and an additional faculty examiner.

Students are also required to abide by all policies and procedures of TPCB and of the graduate school in which they are formally matriculated.

## After TPCB

Graduates emerge as independent scientists prepared to pursue research at the interface of chemistry and biology, and go on to careers in academia, the pharmaceutical industry, and biotechnology. See where our [alumni]({{ '/alumni/' | relative_url }}) have gone.
