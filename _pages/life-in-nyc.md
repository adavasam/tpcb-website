## Life as a TPCB student

TPCB students form a close-knit group within the broader Tri-Institutional research community. Students arrive on campus during the summer before their first year, which gives them time to settle into the research environment and the neighborhood — and to get a feel for living in New York City — before classes begin in the fall. Orientation events introduce the resources available to them and offer advice on the transition to graduate school and to the city. At the end of the summer, TPCB students also take part in the orientation activities of the broader Weill Cornell graduate programs.

## What the program covers

Students receive a graduate stipend intended to accommodate the cost of living in New York City, so that they can focus on research and still have a life outside the lab. The program also covers all tuition and fees, and provides health insurance for students and their dependent children.

Each student receives an individual research allowance annually, which may be used for travel to scientific conferences, a laptop or tablet computer, scientific textbooks, and other research resources. First-year students receive additional funds toward relocation expenses and official transcript fees.

## Housing

The program provides all students with subsidized housing, either on campus or nearby in the neighborhood, so students live within reach of the labs they work in.

## The neighborhood

TPCB sits on the Upper East Side of Manhattan — a comfortable, safe neighborhood with a community feel, plenty of local dining and shopping, and easy access to the restaurants, entertainment, sporting events and sightseeing of the rest of New York City. Students get the convenience of living near their labs while still taking full advantage of what the city has to offer.

## The social calendar

The TPCB Social Committee hosts gatherings and events throughout the year. It is open to all students, and at its annual meeting volunteer coordinators are appointed for each event and the year's agenda is revised. Recurring events include:

- **First-Year Orientation** — a series of activities welcoming new first-year students to the program.
- **New Year's Party** — a holiday celebration in January for TPCB students and faculty.
- **Summer Barbeque** — a summer gathering over food and drinks.
- **Fun Journal Club** — student presentations on quirky scientific topics, over food and drinks.
- **TPCB Research-In-Progress Seminar Series** — an internal meeting, with students coordinating snacks and scheduling.

The committee is always looking for new activities that bring the community together.
