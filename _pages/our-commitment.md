## A supportive training environment

The Tri-Institutional PhD Program in Chemical Biology has a longstanding commitment to supporting its students. We have zero tolerance for racism or discrimination in any form, and we work actively to combat unconscious bias in our admissions and recruitment processes, in our curriculum and academic evaluations, and at our scientific and social events. Our aim is to help every TPCB student reach their full scientific and professional potential, to keep identifying areas where we can do better, and to act on them. We promote these ideals within TPCB and across the scientific community at large.

## Our track record

We hold regular discussions with our students to obtain their insights and feedback. Those conversations have produced the efforts below, and have made the support we already provide more visible.

### The cost of applying, and of arriving

To reduce the financial burden of applying to graduate school, the program provides proactive application fee waivers to students recruited through pipeline programs, and reimburses the cost of official transcripts and score reports for students who matriculate.

Every TPCB graduate student receives guaranteed stipend support, subsidized housing on or near campus, health insurance, and an annual research allowance that may be used for moving expenses, research supplies including a computer or laptop, and attendance at scientific conferences. [Life in NYC]({{ '/life-in-nyc/' | relative_url }}) has more on housing and the first-year transition.

### Settling into graduate school

The TPCB Student Orientation Committee hosts a series of welcome events over the summer, as students arrive on campus, to ease the move into graduate school and into New York City.

In **2021** the program launched TPCB Peer Mentoring, which pairs each incoming student with a senior student mentor for personalized advice and support. In **2022** we introduced training in "hidden curriculum" topics, delivered through workshops and through our courses.

### International students

TPCB students come from around the world. We work closely with graduate school staff so that information specific to international students is available at our recruiting and onboarding events. In **2022** we established the TPCB International Students Group, open to all TPCB students, which provides additional support, peer mentorship, and social events celebrating our students' backgrounds.

### Mental health and wellness

Students face challenges and stresses both within and beyond their graduate studies. The three institutions provide extensive mental health and wellness support, including free on-site counseling, referrals to medical professionals, and access to a range of wellness programs.

### Students who are parents

Childcare and parenting support is provided through the three institutions for students balancing family responsibilities with their scientific training. That includes dedicated lactation rooms for nursing mothers, institutional childcare centers and backup care, help finding babysitters, nannies and child enrichment programs, and information on New York City public and private schools.

### Mentorship

All TPCB faculty members are required to complete mentorship training provided by their institution. Faculty at Memorial Sloan Kettering and Weill Cornell Medicine complete a four-hour course based on content from CIMER, the Center for the Improvement of Mentored Experiences in Research; faculty at The Rockefeller University complete an online module created by CITI, the Collaborative Institutional Training Initiative. Both cover the roles and responsibilities of mentors and mentees, aligning expectations between them, maintaining effective communication, promoting professional development, and providing a healthy and supportive work environment. Faculty are further encouraged to attend the ongoing mentor training workshops and seminars organized across the three campuses.

Students are given resources of their own to make the most of those relationships, inside TPCB and beyond it. Incoming students are paired with a faculty First-Year Advisor, who helps them choose rotations, a thesis lab and coursework, and establish an Individual Development Plan. Every student then maintains a thesis committee of at least four TPCB faculty members and meets with it regularly, for scientific training and professional development alike.

### A culture of outreach

All TPCB students are strongly encouraged to take part in outreach programs. Our students are outstanding role models for young scientists and have taken leadership roles in the Tri-Institutional Outreach Committee, which runs STEM outreach for K–12 through undergraduate students. They also lead and participate in the Weill Cornell/MSK High School Catalyst program, RockEDU Science Outreach, Genspace, and programs at the New York Academy of Sciences. See [Outreach]({{ '/outreach/' | relative_url }}) for what those look like in practice.

## Our ongoing commitment

We evaluate the effectiveness of these policies and activities continuously. The program conducts an annual TPCB Climate Survey and reviews the results with students at the TPCB Student Town Hall each year; the results are also shared with institutional leadership and with the TPCB faculty. Those discussions surface issues that need further attention, and the TPCB leadership team is accountable for ensuring they are addressed. We also engage in frequent, active discussion with the academic leadership of the three institutions, so that our students are provided with the support they need to thrive.

We seek to be leaders in the field at promoting a supportive environment in science.
