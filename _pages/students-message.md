## Campus life in the midst of New York City

We are delighted that you are considering TPCB for your graduate studies.

What drew us here was the consortium of research labs the program opens up. The three institutions are close enough together that moving between them is easy, and that proximity is what makes collaborations — often translational ones — actually happen rather than merely get discussed. The program encourages us to rotate across all three campuses, so we can explore genuinely different research areas: cryo-electron microscopy, total synthesis, drug discovery and design.

We are also enthusiastic about the research-based curriculum. It lets us take only the courses that matter for a thorough understanding of our own scientific interests, and the absence of a teaching requirement means we can concentrate fully on our laboratory projects. That freedom is a large part of why students here are able to produce high-quality, breakthrough research.

Then there is the location. The three institutions cluster together on the Upper East Side of Manhattan, forming a large biomedical center that nonetheless feels like a tight-knit community — a small oasis in a bustling city, where you run into fellow students at the various student and faculty clubs or end up studying together in one of the many lounges across the Tri-I campuses.

We take advantage of the rest of the city too. Museums, restaurants, bars, parks: New York is a place where you can have experiences that last a lifetime. Thanks to the program's generous stipend and subsidized housing, we can afford to live in Manhattan, in good housing on the Upper East Side within easy reach of all three institutions. Both in the laboratory and out on the town, you will find life at TPCB rewarding and exciting.

We hope you will consider joining us.

*The TPCB Student Organizing Committee*
