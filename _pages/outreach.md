## A culture of science outreach

TPCB students are engaged in a wide range of outreach programs that bring the wonder and power of science to all communities and support the next generation of scientists. Most of the work below was started and is run by students themselves.

## Tri-Institutional Outreach Committee (TOrC)

TOrC aims to make science more accessible: spreading awareness about careers in science, inspiring others to follow their passion in STEM, and giving them resources to help them excel. In parallel, it connects Weill Cornell Medicine, Memorial Sloan Kettering and The Rockefeller University by bringing together members of the Tri-I who share an interest in outreach and mentorship.

## Tri-I Mentorship Initiative (TIMI)

In 2020, TOrC student leaders Ifé Akano, Gianna Stella, Ilana Kotliar, Maria Sirenko and Alexa Strauss founded the Tri-I Mentorship Initiative to support people applying to graduate programs through one-on-one mentoring. Current Tri-I graduate students are paired with undergraduates or recent graduates who are applying to biomedical PhD programs. Mentors are trained, and draw on their own experience of the application process to help mentees choose where to apply, craft a personal statement, and improve their interviewing.

Since it began in 2020, TIMI has matched **over 400 mentees from over 25 countries** with graduate student mentors drawn from every PhD program across the Tri-I. Its mentees have received offers from nearly 70 different PhD programs.

## Tri-Institutional Outreach Fair

TOrC launched the Tri-Institutional Outreach Fair in February 2022 to make STEM outreach opportunities in New York City more visible. Nearly 70 members of the Tri-I community came together virtually to hear from 12 professional and trainee-run outreach organizations — among them BioBus, the New York Academy of Sciences Afterschool STEM Mentoring Program, RockEDU, and the Weill Cornell Graduate School's Advancing Cornell Career Experiences for Science Students (ACCESS) — and to meet outreach representatives directly in breakout sessions.

A survey afterwards found that 90% of participants had learned about at least one new outreach group and 50% signed up for a new volunteer opportunity. The Outreach Fair is now an annual event, held each spring.

## High School Catalyst Program

TPCB students have taken on leadership roles in the High School Catalyst Program, a partnership between the Weill Cornell Graduate School and the MSK Postdoctoral Association that offers hands-on biomedical research experience to New York high school students from all backgrounds. The seven-week program pairs each mentee with a mentor who is a current graduate student, and runs an extensive schedule of seminars, workshops, journal clubs and discussions, culminating in an NSF-style research proposal.

Its Co-Presidents are [Charles Warren]({{ '/students/charles-warren/' | relative_url }}) (TPCB, Geri lab) and Noah Yardeny (TPCB, Geri lab), and other TPCB students serve on the leadership team.

## Lab visits with Genspace

TPCB students have extended their outreach into the greater New York City area by organizing lab visits for aspiring young scientists. In collaboration with Genspace, a community biology lab in Sunset Park, Brooklyn, TPCB students lead tours of Tri-I labs for groups of high school and early college students, who learn about the scientific opportunities here and what it is like to be a researcher. The program is led by TPCB student [Jaina Wollowitz]({{ '/students/jaina-wollowitz/' | relative_url }}) (Heller lab).
