## Learn science by doing science

The Tri-Institutional PhD Program in Chemical Biology was established in {{ site.data.program.founding_year }} as one of the first graduate programs in the world to focus on research and training at the interface of chemistry and biology. It is a collaborative offering of three premier New York City institutions — [Weill Cornell Medicine](https://www.med.cornell.edu/), [The Rockefeller University](https://www.rockefeller.edu/), and [Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center](https://www.mskcc.org/) — which sit adjacent to one another in the heart of Manhattan's Upper East Side. Together they form a single university-scale environment with a breadth of scientific opportunity that no one campus could offer on its own.

## Training the next generation of scientific leaders

TPCB students are engaged in forefront research from their first months in the program, mentored by internationally recognized faculty — eminent senior scientists and rising junior investigators alike. Students choose a research area based on their own skills and interests: the organic synthesis of bioactive small molecules, mechanistic investigation of macromolecules, the dissection of cellular processes with chemical probes, the evaluation of novel therapeutics *in vivo*. Because the program sits inside a biomedical research environment, students have access to the full spectrum of fundamental through translational science.

That emphasis on laboratory research shows up in the publication record. TPCB students publish in leading journals throughout their training, averaging {{ site.data.program.avg_publications }} papers per graduate.

Browse the [faculty directory]({{ '/faculty/' | relative_url }}) to see who is taking students, or read about the [program of study]({{ '/program-of-study/' | relative_url }}) to see how the training is structured.

## Resources and community

Students have access to state-of-the-art research laboratories, core facilities, and collaborative research centers across the three campuses, along with courses and seminars created specifically for TPCB and additional coursework at neighboring institutions in New York City. Several lecture series bring world-class scientists to campus, and students interact with them directly rather than from the back of a hall.

Much of the program's calendar is run by the students themselves. They organize and host the annual [Tri-Institutional Chemical Biology Symposium]({{ '/symposium/' | relative_url }}) and the annual TPCB Student Career Development Retreat, and each year the second-year class selects, invites, and hosts a speaker in the flagship Sanders Tri-Institutional Chemical Biology Seminar Series. Students have also built the program's [science outreach]({{ '/outreach/' | relative_url }}) efforts from the ground up.

The program is supported by an NIH Chemistry–Biology Interface T32 Training Grant.

## Fellowships and support

All TPCB students receive a graduate fellowship that includes a stipend, subsidized housing close to campus, healthcare benefits, and an annual research allowance that covers travel to scientific conferences. Students do not teach and are not responsible for securing their own funding, though every student submits at least one [external fellowship application]({{ '/external-fellowships/' | relative_url }}) during their training.

Being centrally located in New York City is part of the offer too — see [life in NYC]({{ '/life-in-nyc/' | relative_url }}) for what that looks like day to day.

## Where our graduates go

The program attracts top students from around the world and has a long-standing commitment to a supportive training environment. Comprehensive career development training runs alongside the research, and graduates have gone on to faculty positions at leading universities, research roles at major pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, and careers in science policy and beyond. Our [{{ site.data.alumni | size }} alumni]({{ '/alumni/' | relative_url }}) are the clearest account of what the program produces.
