TPCB is pleased to welcome five new faculty members, **Kaavya Krishna Kumar**, **Andres Leschziner**, **Samara Reck-Peterson**, **Max Wilkinson**, and **Xiaolan Zhao**, to the program. Their research leverages chemical biology approaches and concepts to address fundamental questions spanning signal transduction, cytoskeletal dynamics, organelle trafficking, RNA biology, and genome stability, bringing exciting new opportunities to our students for multidisciplinary training.

- **Prof. Kaavya Krishna Kumar** recently launched her lab at Weill Cornell, investigating the molecular mechanisms by which G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) sense extracellular ligands and transmit those signals to intracellular pathways. Using structural biology, biophysics, biochemistry, and protein engineering, her group aims to understand how receptor conformations, accessory proteins, and ligand diversity combine to specify signaling outcomes. A central goal is to translate these mechanistic insights into chemical probes and therapeutics for cancer, diabetes, and immunological disease. Prof. Krishna Kumar completed her PhD at the University of Sydney, a research position at the Walter & Eliza Hall Institute, and postdoctoral training at Stanford University. [Read more](https://chembio.triiprograms.org/faculty-research/faculty-directory/kaavya-krishna-kumar-phd/)

- **Prof. Andres Leschziner** studies the structural basis of macromolecular dynamics, combining cryo-electron microscopy with biochemical, biophysical, and cell biological approaches. Long-standing research programs in his lab focus on the mechanism and regulation of the microtubule-associated motor protein dynein, and the factors that regulate chromatin structure and remodeling. More recently, his lab has studied LRRK2, a large kinase whose mutations are among the most common genetic causes of familial Parkinson’s disease, with a central goal of identifying new vulnerabilities that could be exploited for therapeutics. Prof. Leschziner began his academic career at Harvard University, moved to the University of California, San Diego in 2015, and was recruited to Weill Cornell in 2025. [Read more](https://chembio.triiprograms.org/faculty-research/faculty-directory/andres-leschziner-phd/)

- **Prof. Samara Reck-Peterson** joined Weill Cornell Medicine in 2025 as the inaugural Chair of the newly established Department of Biochemistry & Biophysics, and is a Howard Hughes Medical Institute Investigator. Her lab investigates intracellular transport mechanisms spanning molecular to organismal scales. One major focus is on understanding the role of the kinase LRRK2 in Parkinson’s disease, by developing chemical probes and other tools to dissect LRRK2 functions. A complementary program uses genomic, proteomic, and metabolomic approaches to understand how organelle trafficking shapes natural product production in filamentous fungi. Prior to being recruited to Weill Cornell, Prof. Reck-Peterson was a Professor at Harvard Medical School and at the University of California, San Diego. Read more at: [TPCB](https://chembio.triiprograms.org/faculty-research/faculty-directory/samara-reck-peterson-phd/) · [Weill Cornell News](https://biochem.weill.cornell.edu/news-and-events/dr-samara-reck-peterson-named-chair-department-biochemistry-and-biophysics-weill)

- **Prof. Max Wilkinson** recently joined the faculty at MSK in October 2025. His lab seeks to discover and characterize new RNA-based molecular machines using chemical biology, bioinformatics, microbiology, molecular biology, and cryo-electron microscopy. In particular, they seek to uncover previously unknown functions of reverse transcriptases in bacteria. While reverse transcriptases are best known from retroviruses such as HIV, they have shown that reverse transcription activities also underlie fundamental phenomena including RNA splicing and telomere extension, and also appear to mediate phage defense by uncharacterized mechanisms. Prof. Wilkinson completed PhD at the the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology at the University of Cambridge and postdoctoral training at the Broad Institute. [Read more](https://chembio.triiprograms.org/faculty-research/faculty-directory/max-wilkinson-phd/)

- **Prof. Xiaolan Zhao** is a leader in studying the cellular mechanisms that preserve genome stability. Her lab studies DNA replication, DNA repair, and cellular responses to DNA damage, processes that ensure faithful inheritance of genetic information. By combining chemical biology, genetic, biochemical, structural, single-molecule, and genomic strategies, her group investigates how cells protect their genomes during normal growth and under conditions of stress, and how failures in these pathways contribute to cancer, aging, and other human diseases. Prof. Zhao has been an MSK faculty member since 2005. [Read more](https://chembio.triiprograms.org/faculty-research/faculty-directory/xiaolan-zhao-phd/)
