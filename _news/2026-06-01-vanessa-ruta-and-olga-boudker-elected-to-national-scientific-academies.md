TPCB congratulates faculty members **Vanessa Ruta** and **Olga Boudker** on their recent elections to two of the nation’s most prestigious honorary societies. Prof. Ruta at Rockefeller University was elected to the National Academy of Sciences and Prof. Boudker at Weill Cornell Medicine was elected to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. These honors recognize their outstanding scientific achievements and lasting contributions to the fields of neuroscience, biophysics, and chemical biology. With these elections, the TPCB faculty now includes 11 members of the National Academy of Sciences and 9 members of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

- **Prof. Vanessa Ruta** is Gabrielle H. Reem and Herbert J. Kayden Professor at Rockefeller University and a Howard Hughes Medical Institute Investigator. The Ruta laboratory has transformed our understanding of how neural circuits generate behavior. Using the fruit fly *Drosophila melanogaster* as a model system, her research explores how innate and learned behaviors arise from neural activity and how these circuits are modified by experience and evolution. Her work has provided fundamental insights into olfactory learning and memory, neuromodulation, and the neural mechanisms that allow the same sensory cue to drive different behavioral responses depending on experience, context, or evolutionary variation. Her work has also revealed the structural basis of odor detection, defining how insect odorant receptors recognize chemical cues and convert them into neural activity that guides behavior. Prof. Ruta’s election recognizes her pioneering contributions to neuroscience and her innovative approaches to understanding how brains generate adaptive behavior. The National Academy of Sciences is one of the world’s most prestigious professional societies. Scientists are elected by their peers to membership for outstanding contributions to research and a consensus within the scientific community that an individual is a national leader in their field. - **Prof. Olga Boudker** is a Professor of Biochemistry and Biophysics at Weill Cornell Medicine and a Howard Hughes Medical Institute Investigator. Prof. Boudker is internationally recognized for her groundbreaking studies of membrane transport proteins, molecular machines that move nutrients and neurotransmitter molecules across cellular membranes. Her laboratory combines structural biology, cryo-electron microscopy, and advanced biophysical approaches to uncover how these transporters function at the molecular level. She elucidated the “elevator” mechanism used by glutamate transporters, proteins that play critical roles in neural communication and whose dysfunction has been linked to neurological disorders including epilepsy, Alzheimer’s disease, and stroke. Her election recognizes her influential discoveries that have advanced our understanding of membrane protein structure and function. The American Academy of Arts & Sciences is one of the United States’ oldest honorary societies, chartered to recognize Americans with significant accomplishments.
