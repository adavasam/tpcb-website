TPCB congratulates **Yuanhuang Chen**, **Michelle Guo**, and **Kaila Nishikawa**, who recently received competitive graduate fellowships in recognition of their outstanding research. These awards support innovative chemical biology dissertation projects spanning cancer biology, immunology, and epigenetics and highlight the breadth of research being carried out by our trainees.

- **Yuanhuang Chen** was awarded a Predoctoral Fellowship in Drug Discovery from the PhRMA Foundation for his project entitled, “Elucidating the Role of Cysteinyl Leukotrienes (CysLTs) in Cancer and Aging”. Yuanhuang is carrying out his thesis research with Prof. Thomas Sakmar at Rockefeller, where he is investigating how CysLTs, pro-inflammatory lipid mediators secreted by aged epidermal cells, regulate melanocyte cell fate by promoting either proliferation or senescence. His work has important implications for understanding melanoma biology and identifying new therapeutic strategies for melanoma and pigmentation disorders. Read more at: [PhRMA](WWWWW)

- **Ruiyang (Michelle) Guo** received a fellowship from the MSK Kravis WiSE program for her project entitled, “Mechanism and Role of Regulated Protein Turnover during Cytotoxic T Lymphocyte Activation”. Michelle is pursuing her thesis research jointly with Prof. Heeseon An and Prof. Morgan Huse at MSK, where she is investigating how regulated protein turnover shapes cytotoxic T lymphocyte activation and function. By uncovering mechanisms governing protein degradation during immune responses, her work seeks to deepen our understanding of T cell biology and may ultimately inform future immunotherapeutic strategies. Read more at: [MSK](https://www.sloankettering.edu/gerstner/student-life/commencement/2026-mskcc-academic-convocation-and-louis-gerstner-jr-graduate-school-of-biomedical-sciences-commencement)

- **Kaila Nishikawa** was honored with a Doris J. Hutchison Graduate Fellowship at MSK for her project entitled, “Expanding the Epitranscriptome: Linking Non-enzymatic RNA Modifications in States of Hyperglycemia to Disease Progression”. Kaila is carrying out her thesis research with Prof. Yael David at MSK, where she is exploring how elevated glucose levels can drive previously underappreciated non-enzymatic modifications to RNA. By investigating how these RNA changes arise and influence cellular function, her research aims to reveal new molecular links between metabolic dysfunction and disease progression. Read more at: [MSK](https://www.sloankettering.edu/gerstner/student-life/commencement/2026-mskcc-academic-convocation-and-louis-gerstner-jr-graduate-school-of-biomedical-sciences-commencement)
