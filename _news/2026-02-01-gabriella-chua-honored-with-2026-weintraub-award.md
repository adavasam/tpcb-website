**Gabriella Chua, PhD**, a recent TPCB graduate, has been recognized with a **2026 Harold M. Weintraub Graduate Student Award**! The award is named in honor of Prof. Hal Weintraub, a pioneering molecular biologist at the Fred Hutch Cancer Center. The award has been sponsored annually by the center since 2000, and awardees are selected by a committee of Fred Hutch faculty and students. Gabriella’s thesis research with **Prof. Shixin Liu** at Rockefeller focused on uncovering new molecular mechanisms in DNA replication and chromatin regulation using single-molecule techniques. In her early studies, she and TPCB colleagues **Rachel Leicher** (Liu Lab), **Adewola Osunsade** (David Lab, MSK), and postdoctoral fellow **Sarah Faulkner** (David Lab) uncovered that linker histone H1, a key component of chromatin, binds and forms condensates with single-stranded DNA, suggesting new roles for H1 in DNA damage repair. This work was published in *Nature Structural & Molecular Biology* in 2022. Gabriella next conceived and initiated a new project examining the molecular behavior of MeCP2 (methyl-CpG-binding protein 2), a major epigenetic regulator that is mutated in Rett syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder currently lacking a cure. Using single-molecule observation and manipulation, she found that MeCP2 preferentially binds and mechanically stabilizes nucleosomes within chromatin, establishing a new biophysical framework for understanding the biological function of MeCP2 and the aberrant behavior of its disease-associated mutants. This research was also published in *Nature Structural & Molecular Biology* in 2024. Finally, in the later stages of her PhD, Gabriella turned her attention to PCNA (proliferating cell nuclear antigen), a master coordinator of eukaryotic DNA replication. By visualizing the dynamic interaction between PCNA and its loader protein called RFC (replication factor C) in real time, she discovered that, contrary to expectations, RFC remains bound to PCNA after the DNA loading step, and the two proteins move together along DNA, an interaction that is important for the DNA replication process. This work identified a new role for RFC in DNA synthesis that overturns the canonical model of replicative clamp loading and was published in *Cell* this month.

Prof. Liu commented that “Gabriella has seemingly boundless curiosity and intellectual capacity. She independently conceived of and led two major projects while also collaborating with other groups on several other projects.” He also noted, “She also advocates for equity in science, support for underserved communities, and improving mentorship practices,” citing in particular a discussion series that she established at Rockefeller, called *Unveiling the Hidden Curriculum for Asian Trainees*. TPCB Director Derek Tan concurred, “Gabriella is a fantastic scientist and has also been a leader in our scientific community.” She co-chaired our Tri-I Chemical Biology Symposium in 2021 and led the TPCB Student Organizing Committee in 2021–22. She has also participated in numerous teaching and mentoring roles at the New York Academy of Sciences and co-chaired the High School Catalyst outreach program at Weill Cornell. In 2023, she wrote an important perspective in *ChemBioChem* on “Students’ Perspectives on Scientific Training” with TPCB colleagues **J. Jeya Vandana** and **Chun-Cheng Hsieh**. Gabriella was recognized for her leadership with the TPCB Student Service Award in 2024. She is now a postdoctoral fellow with Prof. Michael O’Donnell at Rockefeller.
